### Added

- **Canvas**: open `.canvas` files (JSON Canvas, as Obsidian writes them) in a canvas editor, or make one with "New canvas" (Ctrl+Alt+C). Double-click to add a card, drag from a card's side to connect it, drag cards to move them (onto the grid if you like), drag a corner to resize, drag across the canvas to select several. Cards show their Markdown; note cards show the note's first lines, image cards the picture, PDF cards the first page, and Mermaid cards the diagram (full edition). Group cards, colour them, line them up or space them evenly, copy and paste them (also between canvases), and undo and redo. Scroll to move around, Ctrl+scroll or pinch to zoom.
- **Drawing**: a pen (which follows a stylus's pressure), marker, highlighter, eraser and shapes (rectangle, ellipse, line, arrow) on a canvas, or in a note with "Insert sketch". Drawings are saved as SVG files that any app can show, and stay editable in Quire.
- Canvas files keep everything Quire doesn't know about, and are saved one card per line, so sync, version history and agents' suggestions work card by card. A canvas changed elsewhere while open is updated, and your unsaved changes are merged in.
- AI apps can read and change canvases over MCP (`canvas_read`, `canvas_apply`). Outside agent folders their changes are suggestions; reviewing one shows what each card change does ("Add text node …").
- **PDFs**: their text is searchable, and links like `[[paper.pdf#page=3]]` open them at that page. Scanned pages are read with on-device OCR when it's turned on.
- **PDF viewer** (full edition): continuous pages, zoom, find in the PDF, select and copy text, and working links. Highlight text or an area (a figure, a table) in five colours and add a comment. Highlights are kept in a Markdown note next to the PDF (`paper (annotations).md`), can be linked to with `[[paper.pdf#page=3&annot=h7f3a]]`, and are found again when the PDF is replaced by a new version (those that can't be found are listed as detached). Quire Lite shows the PDF's text and its highlights, and opens it in your system's viewer.
- **Citations**: write `[@key]`, `[@key, p. 4]`, `[see @a; @b]` or `@key` with works from a BibTeX library (`references.bib`, or files named in Settings → Citations). Typing `[@` suggests works; the status bar shows the work under the caret, and Ctrl+click opens its PDF in the vault or its entry. Preview, HTML export and publishing show "(Lovelace 1843)" and a list of references; the PDF export (full edition) uses APA, Chicago or IEEE.
- "Import bibliography…" takes a `.bib` file or Zotero's CSL-JSON export, shows what it would add or change, and merges it into the library by key. It can keep watching the file (for a Better BibTeX auto-export) and merge its changes.
